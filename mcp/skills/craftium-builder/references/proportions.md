# Proportions: Minecraft scale, not real-world scale

One block is about 1 m, but real dimensions look wrong here. The player is 2 blocks tall, the smallest detail is a
whole block, and a first-person camera makes real-sized rooms feel low and thin. Builds read well when the BIG
things are exaggerated and the SMALL things are held at their fixed size.

## Two kinds of elements

- FIXED, never scale: doors (1x2, double 2x2), single stairs and steps, beds, fences, panes, trapdoors. Everything
  else is sized so these look right.
- SCALABLE, exaggerate: rooms, walls, columns, roofs, towers. Taller and chunkier than real life.
- Round every real feature UP to at least one block (trim, sills, ledges). Exaggerating is correct. Dropping the
  feature leaves a flat wall.

## What is usual (guidance, not rules)

These numbers are what is typical, not what is required. Treat them as a starting guess, and let the brief, the
reference image, the site and your own judgement override them. Never apply a number just because it is listed.
When you choose one, or depart from one, say so in a line to the player.

Where each came from matters, so weigh it accordingly.

### Sourced: the Minecraft Wiki roof guidelines (stated rules)

- Roof pitch follows the block: double blocks about 63 deg, stairs 45 deg, alternating slabs about 22 deg. Pick the
  pitch first and the roof height follows from the width.
- A roof is usually 1 to 2 storeys tall on an inhabited building.
- Past about 15x15, one roof looks like a barn. Use several roof sections and clear the blocks inside the V where
  they join.
- Match width parity to the centre feature: one door or window needs an odd width (the peak sits over it); a double
  door needs an even width.

### Measured: 11 hand-built biome builds, all one style family

Large builds, about 20 to 35 blocks on a side (median about 27 by 27) and 12 to 45 tall. Typical of big ornate
builds, NOT of every build. Small cottages will differ.

- Storeys were about 6 to 8 blocks floor to floor (5 to 8 across the set, 4 to 5 in one). The wiki's example uses 4
  to 5. A smaller build usually wants less. Choose from the size and mood of the build.
- Roofs rose 8 to 15 blocks above the eaves, about as tall as the walls or taller.
- Where roofs slope, 45 deg stair roofs dominate. The steeper 2:1 slope is uncommon. Desert and ocean builds had
  mostly flat roofs.
- Stairs and slabs made 21 to 44 percent of the blocks, and detail blocks (stairs, slabs, trapdoors, fences, walls,
  lanterns, doors) about 40 to 60 percent. These builds are mostly shaped blocks, not full cubes.
- About 50 to 95 different blocks per build, but 80 percent of all blocks came from only 11 to 24 of them: a few
  dominant materials and many accents.

### Convention: common builder habits with no source or measurement here

- Window sizes (1x2 small, 2x2 or 3x2 large) with 1-block pillars between.
- Eaves overhang of 1 block.
- 60/30/10 split of main, secondary and accent materials.
- Several masses (3 or 4), each secondary mass 1/3 to 1/2 of the main one, with one clear focal point.

### Not known: do not invent a "standard"

- Window rules: 7 of the 11 reference builds had no glass at all.
- Eave overhang.
- How many masses a build should have.

## How to use them

1. Fix the FIXED elements first (door, step, window height).
2. Pick a storey height from the build's size and the brief. A reference image beats these numbers: measure its
   ratios and match them.
3. Pick the roof pitch by block type, then check the height against the wall height, not against a number.
4. Check that one mass is clearly the focal point and the rest are smaller.
5. State your choices in one line, for example "7-block storeys, 13 wide, 45 deg roof", so the player can correct
   them.
