# Landmark room sizes (usual, not rules)

Use this only when the ruleset and the brief give no size. Everything here is a starting guess to adapt, never a
target, and you say when you use or depart from one. If the user's ruleset gives a size, it replaces all of this.

## Facts from the Minecraft Wiki (minecraft.wiki, checked 2026-11-08)

The wiki gives few room dimensions, so these are all it states:

- **Player:** 1.8 blocks tall and 0.6 wide standing, 1.5 tall sneaking. Below 1.5 blocks of headroom the player
  crawls. A jump reaches about 1.25 blocks, so a 1-block step can be climbed and a 2-block step cannot.
- **Monster room (dungeon):** generated in an open area 7 or 9 blocks wide and long and 6 high, with 1 to 5 openings
  2 high at floor level. The wiki's own example is a 5 by 5 room, so the inside is the area less its walls (an
  inference: the page does not give the inside size).
- **Desert pyramid:** a 21 by 21 ground floor, with a 5 by 5 underground room.
- **Stronghold:** a straight corridor is 5 blocks long; the storeroom crossing has 2 floors, each 2 blocks high.
- **Jungle temple, pillager outpost, village houses:** the wiki gives no room sizes (the outpost is "a 4-story
  watchtower"). Do not quote vanilla sizes for them.

## Usual inside sizes (building habit, NOT from the wiki)

These come from general Minecraft building habits. They are unverified, so adapt freely.

| Room kind | Inside (w x d x h) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Closet, secret space | 1 to 3 wide, 2 to 3 deep, 3 high | a hiding place |
| Small chamber (guard room, cell) | 5 to 7 x 5 to 7 x 4 | room for one spawner and a chest or two; matches the monster room |
| Large hall (burial chamber, temple nave) | 9 to 13 x 9 to 15 x 5 to 7 | space to move and see the contents |
| Boss room | 11 to 17 x 11 to 17 x 6 to 9 | a fight needs room and height |
| Entrance, doorway | 1 to 3 wide x 2 to 3 high | 2 high fits a standing player |

- A room under 3 blocks high feels like a crawlspace. A tower storey is usually 4 to 5 blocks high inside, with the
  floor and ceiling adding 1 each.
- A temple room is often taller than a dungeon room because height reads as importance.
- A room has to hold its contents: a chest and a spawner are 1 block each, but the player needs space to reach them.
